Shower remodels in Tucson

Custom walk-in showers with tile, benches, niches and low or curbless entries, built from the waterproofing out.

A shower built around how you use it

Most shower remodels in Tucson start the same way: a dated tub and shower combo or a cramped fiberglass stall, and a homeowner who wants something bigger, easier to step into and easier to clean. A custom walk-in shower is built in place, so the size, tile, bench, niches and glass fit your room instead of a stock insert.

AZ Atlas Builder Group is a family-owned, licensed general contractor in Tucson (AZ ROC #344537). Custom showers are part of our bathroom remodeling work, and we take them from demolition to the final grout line with one point of contact.

What goes into a custom walk-in shower

Waterproofing first

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. What keeps water out of your walls and floor is the layer behind the tile: a properly sloped shower pan, a waterproof membrane on the walls and floor, and sealed seams around the drain, niches and valve. It's the part you never see and the part that matters most. Shortcuts here are why so many older showers end up with soft drywall, stained baseboards or a musty smell.

Tile and layout

Large-format porcelain, marble-look tile, mosaic floors, a feature wall: almost any look is possible. Bigger tiles mean fewer grout lines to clean, while smaller tiles on the floor give more grip and follow the slope to the drain more easily. We plan the layout so cuts land in sensible places and niches line up with the tile.

Benches and niches

A built-in bench gives you somewhere to sit, shave or rest a foot. Recessed niches keep bottles off the floor. Both have to be framed and waterproofed as part of the shower, not added later, so we settle their size and position before any tile goes up.

Curbless and low-curb entries

A curbless shower has no step at all. The bathroom floor runs straight in and slopes to a linear or center drain. It looks clean and is easier for anyone with mobility concerns. On a concrete slab, going curbless usually means cutting and recessing the slab so the shower floor can sit lower than the rest of the room, which adds work. A low curb is often a good middle ground, and we'll tell you honestly which suits your bathroom.

Glass, valves and fixtures

Fixed glass panels, a hinged door or a doorless walk-in. A rain head plus a handheld on a slide bar. A valve placed so you can turn the water on without standing in the cold spray. Matte black, brushed brass or chrome trim changes the feel of the whole room.

Do you need a permit for a shower remodel in Tucson?

It depends on the scope. The City of Tucson's permit exemptions page says replacing finishes like tile does not need a permit when the work doesn't affect plumbing or electrical. Replacing a sink, shower or tub does need a permit if the work involves connecting new water, drain or vent piping. Moving a drain or relocating the valve usually puts a shower in that second group.

Unincorporated Pima County and the towns of Oro Valley, Marana and Sahuarita run their own permit offices with their own rules. We confirm what your project needs before work starts.

Common shower upgrades in Tucson homes

  • Replacing a tub nobody uses with a larger walk-in shower (see tub to shower conversions)
  • Swapping a fiberglass insert for a tiled shower with a bench
  • Widening a primary shower and adding fixed glass
  • Rebuilding a leaking shower with proper waterproofing
  • Adding solid blocking in the walls so grab bars can be anchored now or later

How a shower remodel works with us

  1. Site walk. We look at the bathroom, check where the plumbing sits and what the floor is built on, and talk through the look you want.
  2. Written estimate. A clear scope listing tile, glass, fixtures and anything excluded.
  3. Demolition and repairs. If we find water damage or rotted framing behind the old shower, we show you before repairing it. Our own crew handles framing and structural repair.
  4. Waterproofing, tile and glass. Built in the right order, with cure times respected.
  5. Final walk-through. We go over the finished shower with you.
