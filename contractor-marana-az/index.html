Building and remodeling in Marana

Marana stretches northwest of Tucson along I-10, from Continental Ranch and Gladden Farms to Dove Mountain in the Tortolita foothills. Much of it was built in the last 25 years, so a lot of our work here is turning builder-grade homes into something more personal: bigger showers, better kitchens, a casita out back, or an addition for a growing family.

Who issues permits in Marana

Marana is a separate town, so permits come from the Town of Marana's Development Services, not the City of Tucson. Arizona's 2024 ADU law applies to cities over 75,000 people, so casitas in Marana follow the town's own code, and many neighborhoods add HOA rules on top.

Casitas and additions in Marana

Marana is one of the fastest-growing towns in the region, but with fewer than 75,000 residents it isn't covered by Arizona's 2024 ADU law (HB 2720) the way the City of Tucson is. The Town of Marana's own code decides what can go on your lot, and most master-planned neighborhoods add HOA rules on top. We check both before any design work. See our Marana casita page for detail, or our casita cost guide for budgeting.

Common repairs on newer Marana homes

Production homes built in the last 25 years are usually wood-frame with a stucco skin and concrete tile roofs. Over time the heat cycles open hairline stucco cracks, gutters and roof valleys collect debris, and monsoon storms find weak flashing. We handle stucco crack repair, roof leak repair and tile roof repairs, and our monsoon prep guide covers what to check before summer storms.

Building new in Marana

Larger lots on Marana's edges and in the Tortolita foothills are good candidates for a custom home. Our Marana custom home page and custom home cost guide cover what affects price, from utilities to slope.
