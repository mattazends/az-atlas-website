Why a primary suite is one of Tucson's most popular additions

Plenty of Tucson homes were built with a modest main bedroom and a small bathroom. A primary suite addition (what used to be called a master suite) adds a larger bedroom, a real bathroom and a walk-in closet without moving house. It's often the addition that changes daily life the most, because it's the room you start and end every day in.

What goes into a primary suite

The bedroom

Placement matters as much as size. Many owners like a split layout, with the suite on the opposite side of the house from the other bedrooms. We also look at sun: a big west-facing window in a Tucson bedroom can make it the hottest room in the house by late afternoon.

The bathroom

This is where most of the budget goes. Walk-in showers with a bench, double vanities and a freestanding tub are the common wish list. Our bathroom and custom shower work is done by the same team building the addition, so tile, glass and plumbing are planned with the framing, not squeezed in afterward.

The closet

A walk-in closet needs less space than people expect when it's planned with the bathroom. Built-in shelving and drawers from our custom cabinets work can do more than extra square feet.

The hard parts of adding a suite

Foundation and opening the wall

New footings tie into the existing foundation, and caliche can slow the dig. Where the addition joins the house, part of an exterior wall usually comes out. If that wall carries load, a beam goes in, which is structural work we do in house.

Roof tie-in

The new roof has to join the old one without leaks, whether you have concrete tile, shingle or a flat roof with parapets. It's the single most important detail on any addition, and our crew handles the roofing itself.

Plumbing runs

A new bathroom needs drain lines that slope to your sewer or septic line. If the suite is far from existing plumbing, that run gets longer. On septic lots in county areas, adding a bedroom can mean the system needs a check first.

Cooling

Your existing AC may not have capacity for the extra square feet. Many suite additions get their own mini-split, which also lets you keep the bedroom cooler at night without cooling the whole house.

Stucco match

Matching stucco texture and color is what makes an addition look original. We do the stucco ourselves.

Permits and approvals

A primary suite addition needs a building permit. Inside the City of Tucson, the residential permits page lists additions among the projects that need plans, a permit and inspections. Pima County and the towns of Oro Valley, Marana and Sahuarita issue their own, and many HOAs need to approve the design first. If you live in one of Tucson's historic preservation zones, exterior changes also go through historic design review.

Questions to answer before design

  • Tub, shower or both? A large walk-in shower often fits where a tub and a small shower would each feel cramped.
  • One sink or two? A double vanity needs wall length, so it shapes the bathroom footprint.
  • Toilet room? A separate water closet adds privacy and a door, and a little space.
  • Access from outside? A door to a patio or courtyard is a favorite in Tucson, and it affects where the suite sits on the lot.
  • Future needs? A curbless shower and blocking for grab bars cost little now and help later.

Settling these early keeps the plan tight and stops expensive changes once framing starts.

Out, up or a casita?

Most Tucson suite additions go out, onto the yard. If your lot is tight, a second-story addition is possible but needs structural checks. If the suite is really for a parent who wants independence, a mother-in-law suite or detached casita may fit better. Our casita vs home addition guide compares them, and the home addition cost guide explains what moves the price.
