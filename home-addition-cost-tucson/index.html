Home addition cost in Tucson: what really drives the price

A bedroom, primary suite or family room is one of the best ways to get more from the home you already own. Here's what goes into the number.

Plan an Addition or Casita Call

Why additions are priced differently from new space

Per square foot, an addition usually costs more than building the same space from scratch. The reason is the tie-in: opening an exterior wall, joining a new roof to the old one, extending plumbing, power and cooling, and blending stucco so nobody can tell where the old house ends. That tie-in work is where an experienced crew earns its keep.

The biggest cost drivers

What the room is for

A bedroom or family room is mostly framing, drywall and finishes. A primary suite with a walk-in shower, or anything with a kitchen, adds plumbing, tile and cabinetry, which raises the cost per foot.

Foundation and structure

New footings have to tie into the existing foundation. Caliche can slow the dig. If an exterior wall comes out to open the space, a beam may need to carry the load, which is structural work we do in house.

Roof tie-in

Joining a new roof to an existing tile, shingle or flat roof without leaks is the most critical detail of the job. Matching the material matters too. See our roofing page.

Stucco match

New stucco has to match the old texture and color so the addition looks original. It's one of the most visible details on any Tucson home, and one we handle ourselves. See stucco.

Cooling

Your existing AC may not have the capacity for extra square footage in a Tucson summer. Many additions get their own mini-split.

Second story or ground floor

Building up saves yard space but means checking the existing walls and foundation can carry the load, which adds engineering and structural work. Most Tucson additions go out, not up.

Permits and HOA

Every addition needs a building permit from your city, town or Pima County, and many neighborhoods need HOA approval first. Our Tucson permits guide lists who issues permits where.

Addition or detached casita?

If the space is for someone who needs privacy, like a parent, an adult child or a tenant, a detached casita can work better. If it's for your own household, an attached addition usually feels more natural. Compare with our casita cost guide.

How we price an addition
