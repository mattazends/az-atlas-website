Bathroom remodel cost in Tucson

Published ballpark prices, the details that push a Tucson bathroom above or below them, and how to get a real number for yours.

Request a Bathroom Estimate Call

The short answer

We couldn't find a credible, current cost survey for Tucson bathrooms alone, so here is the most useful published national figure instead. This Old House's cost guide, updated in 2026, puts the average bathroom remodel at about $15,586, with most projects between $6,456 and $24,715 (source). The same guide puts a full remodel including demolition at around $18,706 on average, and a cosmetic remodel without demolition at around $11,582.

Those are national averages, not Tucson quotes. They're useful for a sense of scale and not much more, because the scope of your bathroom matters far more than the city it's in.

Rule of thumb: anything that moves plumbing, adds tile or uncovers water damage pushes the price up. Keeping fixtures where they are and choosing simpler tile keeps it down.

What drives the price of a Tucson bathroom remodel

Refresh or full gut

New paint, a vanity, fixtures and a toilet in the same spots is a very different job from taking the room back to studs. Inside the City of Tucson, finishes like tile, cabinets and countertops don't need a permit when plumbing and electrical aren't affected, per the city's permit exemptions page. Once new water, drain or vent piping is connected, a permit is needed, and the cost and schedule grow.

The shower

A custom tiled shower with a bench, niches and glass is usually the biggest single line in a bathroom remodel. Curbless entries cost more again, because a slab floor usually has to be cut and recessed. See our shower remodel page.

Tub decisions

Swapping a tub for a walk-in shower, or adding a freestanding tub, both mean plumbing changes. Our tub to shower conversion page covers what that involves.

Tile: how much and what kind

Floor-to-ceiling tile, large-format slabs and patterned layouts take more material and more labor than a simple wainscot. Natural stone costs more than porcelain and needs sealing.

Vanity and storage

A stock vanity is the budget option. A custom vanity built to fit the room, or a double vanity with drawers, adds cost and usually adds a lot of everyday function. See custom cabinets.

What's behind the walls

Older showers and tubs often hide water damage: rotted studs, a soft subfloor or failed backer board. You can't price it until the wall is open. We show you anything we find before we fix it.

Ventilation, lighting and power

A properly ducted exhaust fan, better lighting and new outlets all involve electrical work, which can bring a permit into the picture.

Accessibility details

Grab-bar blocking costs little during a remodel. A curbless shower, wider doorway or relocated controls cost more but can let you stay in your home longer.

How to keep a bathroom remodel on budget

  • Keep the toilet, sink and shower drain where they are if you can.
  • Spend on the shower and the vanity, which you use every day, and save on accent pieces.
  • Pick porcelain over natural stone if you want the look with less upkeep.
  • Choose tile, fixtures and glass before work starts. Changes mid-job cost the most.
  • Plan a contingency for hidden damage in older showers.

Comparing bathroom estimates

Two estimates for the same bathroom can be thousands of dollars apart and still both be honest, because they describe different jobs. When you compare, line them up on the details that matter:

  • Which waterproofing system goes behind the shower tile, and does it cover the walls and the floor?
  • How far does the tile go: shower only, wainscot, or floor to ceiling?
  • Are fixtures, glass and the vanity included, or are they allowances?
  • Who handles permits, and are fees included?
  • How is hidden damage handled if it's found?
  • Is the contractor licensed? You can check on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors site.
