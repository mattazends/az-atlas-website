How much does it cost to build a casita in Tucson?

The honest answer is “it depends”, so here is exactly what it depends on, and how to get a real number for your lot.

Plan an Addition or Casita Call

The short answer

Published 2026 cost guides put a detached casita of about 600 sq ft in Tucson anywhere from roughly $90,000 for a basic build to around $250,000 for a high-end one (source). That's a wide range because every lot is different. Use it as a ballpark only. Your real price comes from your lot, your plans and your finishes, which is why we price from a site walk, not a formula.

Quick rule of thumb: kitchens and bathrooms are the most expensive square feet in any casita. A studio with one bathroom and a kitchenette costs far less per foot than the same size with a full kitchen and two baths.

What moves the price

Size and layout

Bigger costs more, but not in a straight line. A simple rectangle with one roofline is the most efficient shape to frame, roof and stucco. Every corner, bump-out and roof change adds labor.

Attached or detached

A detached casita needs its own foundation, four exterior walls, its own roof and separate utility runs. An attached addition shares a wall and can often tie into the home's existing plumbing and power, though the tie-in work has its own cost. Our home addition cost guide covers that side.

Utilities and distance

Sewer, water and power have to reach the casita. The farther it sits from your sewer line and electric panel, the more trenching. Older homes may need a panel upgrade, and septic lots in county areas may need the system checked or enlarged before a bedroom can be added.

The ground itself

Caliche, slope and washes are local cost drivers. Hard caliche slows excavation, sloped lots may need more foundation work, and a lot near a wash can need a floodplain review.

Cooling and the roof

Tucson summers mean the casita needs proper insulation and its own cooling, usually a mini-split. Inside the City of Tucson, new casitas also need a cool roof.

Finishes and matching the house

Tile, cabinets, fixtures and flooring swing the budget the most. Matching your home's stucco texture and roof material also matters for value, so it looks original rather than added on.

Plans and permit

Custom drawings cost more than starting from one of the City of Tucson's pre-approved casita plans. Permit and review fees depend on your jurisdiction. See our Tucson casita rules guide for who issues your permit.

How to keep a casita in budget

  • Keep the footprint simple and the roofline single.
  • Place it close to your existing sewer line and electric panel.
  • Group the kitchen and bathroom plumbing on one wall.
  • Consider a pre-approved plan if you're inside the City of Tucson.
  • Spend on what you touch every day (shower, counters, flooring) and save on what you don't.
  • Decide finishes before the build starts. Changes mid-build cost the most.
