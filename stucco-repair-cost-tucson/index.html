Stucco repair cost in Tucson: patch, re-coat or re-stucco?

Stucco repair can be a small afternoon job or a whole-house project. Here's what moves the price, and how to tell which kind of repair your walls need.

The short answer

The price of stucco repair follows the scope, and the scope depends on what's actually wrong with the wall. As a national reference, HomeAdvisor's 2025 cost data puts a typical stucco repair at about $600 to $2,662, minor patching of isolated cracks at roughly $200 to $600, and re-coating or resealing at about $4 to $5 per square foot (source). Those are national figures, not Tucson quotes. Use them as a rough guide only. A real number comes from looking at your walls, which is why we price stucco from a site walk.

The cheapest stucco repair is the early one. A crack sealed in spring costs far less than the wet sheathing behind it after a monsoon or two.

Three levels of stucco repair

1. Patch

Spot repairs: a few cracks, holes left by old light fixtures or satellite dishes, a broken corner, or a damaged strip at the bottom of a wall. The cost depends on how many spots there are and how hard the texture is to blend. Be ready for one thing: a patch almost always shows until the wall is painted or color coated, so budget to finish at least that whole wall.

2. Re-coat

When the stucco underneath is sound but the surface is faded, chalky or covered in fine cracks, the fix is to fill the cracks and put a new finish coat or paint system over the whole wall or house. Arizona home expert Rosie Romero suggests repainting a stucco home every seven to 10 years (source).

3. Re-stucco

When stucco is coming away from the wall, sounds hollow, crumbles, or water has reached the lath and framing, patches won't hold. The old stucco comes off, damaged sheathing or framing gets repaired, and new lath and coats go on. Romero's rule of thumb is that a full re-stucco is usually only needed when stucco is delaminating and pulling off the wall. Our re-stucco and stucco replacement page covers that job in detail.

What drives stucco repair cost in Tucson

Size and access

Square footage is the obvious one. Access matters too: second-story walls, parapets, tight side yards and walls behind mature plants take more setup and sometimes scaffolding.

What's behind the crack

This is the big unknown. If water has been getting in for a while, the sheathing or framing behind the stucco may be soft or rotted. Nobody can price that honestly until the wall is opened, so a good estimate tells you up front how hidden damage will be handled. Because our own crew does structural repair and framing, that work doesn't stall the job while you wait on another trade.

Why it cracked

Heat cycles, settling, expansive soil and water all crack stucco in Tucson. If the cause is a roof leak running down inside the wall, or a sprinkler soaking the base every morning, fixing only the stucco means paying twice. See our guide to stucco crack repair for how to tell causes apart.

Texture and color matching

Smooth, sand and heavier hand-troweled finishes each take a different technique, and older homes often have a texture that has to be matched by hand. Color is harder still, because original stucco fades in the sun. Matching well is skilled work and it is part of the price.

Paint or color coat

Whether you finish with paint, elastomeric coating or an integral color coat changes both the cost and how the wall ages. We explain the options on the re-stucco page.

Roof edges and parapets

A lot of Tucson stucco damage starts where the wall meets the roof: cracked parapet caps, scuppers that dump water down the wall, or failed flashing. Fixing the source may mean some flat roof repair or roof leak repair as part of the stucco job.

How to keep stucco repair costs down

  • Seal cracks before monsoon season, not after. Our monsoon prep checklist covers what to check.
  • Turn sprinkler heads away from walls and keep soil and planters off the stucco.
  • Make sure downspouts and scuppers throw water away from the house.
  • Repair a whole wall at once rather than chasing single cracks for years.
  • Fix the cause (a roof leak, drainage or movement) before you pay for cosmetic work.

Common questions.

It depends on the scope. National 2025 figures from HomeAdvisor put a typical repair around $600 to $2,662 and small crack patches around $200 to $600, but those aren't Tucson quotes. Hidden water damage, access and texture matching move the price most. We give a written estimate after seeing the walls.

Patching is far cheaper when the stucco is still bonded to the wall. Re-stucco only makes sense when stucco is delaminating, crumbling or hiding water damage. We'll tell you honestly which one your house needs.

Stucco fades in the sun and textures vary from house to house, so a fresh patch stands out. The fix is to blend the texture carefully and then paint or color coat the whole wall so the color is even.

Small cracks can be filled and painted, but painting over larger or moving cracks hides the problem without fixing it. Rosie on the House notes that elastomeric paint can fill cracks of 1/16 inch or smaller, while elastomeric caulk is better for wider ones.

Warning signs include cracks that keep getting wider, hollow-sounding or bulging areas, stucco crumbling at the base of a wall, staining below a crack, and doors or windows that start sticking. Any of those is worth a closer look.

Spring is ideal because cracks get sealed before monsoon storms arrive. Repairs can be done most of the year, though very hot afternoons and storm days need planning around.
